# Engineering Structures (ENGSTRUCT) - Guide for Authors & Adaptation Rules

## 1. Journal Profile & Editorial Philosophy

- **Official Name**: *Engineering Structures*
- **Publisher**: Elsevier | **ISSN**: 0141-0296
- **Indexing & Metrics**: JCR Q1, Impact Factor: 5.7 (5-Year IF ~6.0)
- **Subject Category**: Engineering, Civil | Mechanics | Construction & Building Technology
- **Aims & Scope**: Engineering Structures provides a forum for a broad blend of scientific and technical papers to reflect the evolving needs of the structural engineering and structural mechanics community. Focuses on structural dynamics, earthquake engineering, wind engineering, safety, reliability, testing, and computational structural mechanics.
- **Core Editorial Philosophy (Crucial for Acceptance)**:
  - **Generalizable Structural Advance**: The work must demonstrate a clear advancement in structural mechanics, performance, or integrity that is generalizable beyond a single routine case study.
  - **Strict Policy on Corresponding Authorship**: The journal enforces a strict rule permitting **strictly ONE corresponding author**. Submissions listing multiple corresponding authors are returned for revision immediately.
  - Case studies without fundamental behavioral insight or new structural formulations are desk-rejected.

## 3. Mandatory Front Matter Requirements

### 3.1 Highlights (Strict 85-Character Constraint)
- 3 to 5 bullet points.
- Maximum 85 characters per bullet (including spaces and punctuation).
- Must encapsulate structural mechanics insights, experimental findings, or structural defect assessment capabilities.

### 3.2 Abstract
- 150 – 250 words. Standalone paragraph.
- State structural mechanics context, experimental/computational methodology, quantitative structural performance gains, and engineering relevance.

### 3.3 Authorship Rule
- Designate exactly one corresponding author with an official institutional email address.

## 4. Manuscript Structure & Engineering Focus

1. **Introduction**:
   - Structural engineering background (e.g., welded joint integrity, fatigue, stress concentrations, structural health monitoring).
   - Critical review of current analytical, experimental, and nondestructive inspection techniques.
   - Distinct definition of structural mechanics/safety gap.
   - Clear statement of objectives in continuous prose.
2. **Structural Mechanics / Inspection Formulation**:
   - Kinematics, constitutive models, finite-element parameters, or automated visual inspection algorithms.
   - Physical meaning of parameters (joint thickness $t$, weld width $b$, stress intensity factors, defect aspect ratios).
3. **Experimental / Benchmark Testing**:
   - Rigorous physical testing setups (full-scale or coupon-level welded specimens).
   - Optical calibration, instrumentation, and measurement tolerances.
4. **Structural Reliability & Standards Compliance**:
   - Evaluation against structural design codes (AISC, Eurocode 3, AWS D1.1, ISO 5817).
   - Impact of defect geometry on remaining structural capacity or fatigue life.
5. **Conclusion**:
   - Synthesize major behavioral findings and practical recommendations for structural design and inspection engineers.

- **Figures**: Clear vector diagrams showing structural load paths, defect morphologies, or stress distributions ($\ge 300\text{ dpi}$ halftones, $\ge 1000\text{ dpi}$ line drawings).
