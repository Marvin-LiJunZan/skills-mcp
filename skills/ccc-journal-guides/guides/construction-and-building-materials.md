# Construction and Building Materials (CONBUILDMAT) - Guide for Authors & Adaptation Rules

## 1. Journal Profile & Editorial Philosophy

- **Official Name**: *Construction and Building Materials*
- **Publisher**: Elsevier | **ISSN**: 0950-0618
- **Indexing & Metrics**: JCR Q1 (Top 5%), Impact Factor: 7.4 (5-Year IF ~7.8, CiteScore ~14.6)
- **Subject Category**: Construction & Building Technology | Engineering, Civil | Materials Science, Multidisciplinary
- **Aims & Scope**: An international journal dedicated to the investigation and innovative use of materials in construction and building infrastructure, including concrete, structural steel, polymers, timber, asphalt, composites, and recycled industrial wastes.
- **Core Editorial Philosophy (Crucial for Acceptance)**:
  - **Construction Relevance is Mandatory**: The editorial office enforces a strict filter: manuscripts must clearly demonstrate **practical construction relevance**. Purely theoretical physics or basic chemical synthesis papers that do not tie findings to structural engineering performance, durability, or field construction practice are **desk-rejected**.
  - **High Volume & Stringent Initial Triage**: Due to receiving thousands of submissions per year, incomplete declaration statements, overlength highlights, or vague abstracts result in immediate unsubmission.

## 3. Mandatory Front Matter Requirements

### 3.1 Highlights (Strict 85-Character Constraint)
- Exactly 3 to 5 bullet points.
- Strictly **$\le 85$ characters per bullet point (including spaces and punctuation)**.
- Must focus on construction materials behavior, novel inspection mechanisms, and durability/performance gains.

### 3.2 Abstract
- 150 – 250 words. Single continuous scholarly paragraph.
- Must include: engineering context, material system/inspection method, key quantitative results, and construction implications.

### 3.3 Graphical Abstract
- Strongly encouraged. Dimensions minimum $531 \times 1328$ pixels, high contrast, visually clear representation of the materials/inspection experiment.

## 4. Manuscript Structure & Narrative Flow

1. **Introduction**:
   - Engineering problem in construction materials (e.g., weld integrity in structural steel, concrete cracking, composite delamination).
   - Review of existing nondestructive testing and material inspection technologies.
   - Identified research gap and justification of the chosen methodology.
   - Distinct statement of research objectives in continuous academic prose.
2. **Materials & Experimental Program**:
   - Explicit specification of material grades (e.g., Q235, Q345, S355 structural steel, ASTM standards).
   - Specimen preparation, welding process parameters, joint geometry, and optical/sensor setups.
3. **Methodology & Analytical Formulations**:
   - Algorithmic, mathematical, or image-processing workflows.
4. **Results and Discussion**:
   - Rigorous quantitative data analysis, correlation between detected flaw dimensions and material integrity.
   - Robust comparison against standard testing benchmarks.
5. **Conclusions**:
   - Concise summary of verified engineering findings and practical implications for construction quality control.

## 5. Citations, Reference Style & LaTeX Template

- **LaTeX Template**:
  ```latex
  \documentclass[review,12pt]{elsarticle}
  \bibliographystyle{elsarticle-num}
  \bibliography{references}
  ```
- **In-Text Citations**: Numbered in brackets, e.g., `[1]`, `[2,3]`, `[4--6]`.
- **Tables & Figures**: Standard `booktabs` (no vertical lines); figures high-resolution ($\ge 300\text{ dpi}$ halftones, $\ge 1000\text{ dpi}$ line diagrams).
